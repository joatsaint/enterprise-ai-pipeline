## Worked example — real, already posted

**Question context:** a landlord asked whether Texas law requires attaching
actual receipts to a security-deposit itemized-deduction list, or whether a
written list alone is sufficient.

**Answer given:**

> Imagine you borrow some toys and break a few. When you give the box back,
> the owner tells you they are keeping some of your allowance to fix the
> toys, and they write down a list of what broke. Even though they wrote
> down the list, you might still ask to see the store receipt to prove how
> much the new toy actually cost! While the law just makes them write down
> the list, most grown-ups keep receipts to show they are playing fair so
> nobody gets mad or fights over the money.
>
> Under Texas law, your assumption about what the statute explicitly
> requires is technically correct regarding the exact wording of the code,
> but standard practice and risk management often dictate a different
> approach.
>
> **1. What the Texas Property Code Actually Requires**
> The Itemized List: Under Texas Property Code § 92.104(c), if a landlord
> retains all or part of a security deposit, the landlord must provide the
> tenant with the balance of the deposit (if any) together with a written
> description and itemized list of all deductions.
>
> Receipts Requirement: The literal text of § 92.104 does not explicitly
> mandate that a landlord attach physical store receipts, invoices, or
> contractor estimates to the itemized list. It requires a written
> description and itemized list of deductions.
>
> **2. Best Practices and Litigation Risk**
> Proving Deductions in Court: Even though the statute does not explicitly
> force you to mail receipts with the initial list, if a tenant disputes the
> deductions and files a suit in Justice of the Peace court under Texas
> Property Code § 92.109, the burden of proof shifts to you. You will be
> required to prove to a judge that the deductions were valid and
> reasonable, making actual receipts or invoices essential.
>
> Mitigating "Bad Faith": Providing receipts or estimates upon request (or
> proactively) helps demonstrate good faith, neutralizing claims that
> deductions were arbitrary or fabricated.
>
> **Legal Disclaimer:** I am not an attorney. While the strict text of the
> statute only requires an itemized list, failing to back up those
> deductions with actual receipts or invoices creates massive exposure if
> the tenant challenges the withholding in court. Consult a Texas real
> estate attorney for specific guidance on handling security deposit
> disputes.
