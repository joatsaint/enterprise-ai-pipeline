# The Prompt — run your own question through this

This is the actual prompt already live and getting real positive
responses in Ernie Garcia's Texas landlord Facebook group (see
`freebies/texas-landlord-qa/texas-landlord-qa_PROMPT.md` for the original).
Reproduced here as part of the guide, framed explicitly as an
**administrative research assistant**, not a legal advisor — this framing
matters for unauthorized-practice-of-law (UPL) safety, both for the reader
using it and for anyone selling it as a product.

See `DISCLAIMER.md`. This applies to every answer this prompt produces.

---

## How to use it

1. Copy the prompt block below.
2. Paste it into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini, followed immediately by your
   own specific landlord question — Texas-specific, one question at a
   time.
3. Treat every answer as a starting point for your own research, not a
   final legal opinion. Verify anything that will actually go into a
   notice, a court filing, or a decision to remove someone's property with
   a licensed Texas attorney or your local Justice of the Peace court
   clerk.

## The prompt

```
Treat each question as completely stand-alone, use only the information
provided, do not assume anything from previous questions or information is
true for this question. Give me the version with the citations. This is
for the state of Texas, cite the exact sources to answer this question,
verify your answers are accurate from legal documents that are current as
of today's date. Give me the answer as though I am 8 years old as a
summary at the top of your answer, vary the introduction sentence. Include
a disclaimer that this is not legal advice and that I should verify with a
licensed Texas attorney or my local Justice of the Peace court clerk
before acting on anything time-sensitive (notices, court filings, or
handling a tenant's property).
```

## Why the "8-year-old summary" framing works

It's not a gimmick — it's the same reason the FAQ Bank in this guide reads
the way it does. A landlord under stress, mid-eviction, reading dense
statute text, needs the plain-English version *first* so they know
whether the detailed answer even applies to their situation, and the
citation-backed version *second* so they (or their attorney) can verify
it. Every real answer collected for this guide's FAQ Bank was produced by
this exact prompt.

## Safety notes for anyone selling or distributing this prompt

- Never let the prompt or its output claim to *be* an attorney or to give
  a "legal opinion" — it must stay framed as a research/administrative
  assistant.
- Every answer must carry the disclaimer (built into the prompt itself
  above, and restated in `DISCLAIMER.md`).
- The date-sensitivity instruction ("current as of today's date") matters
  — Texas Property Code sections do get amended; don't let an old cached
  answer be treated as current law.
